# Paryushan Parv in Jainism: Meaning, Rituals, and a Student-Friendly Guide

Paryushan Parv (also called Paryushan Parva) is one of the most sacred and transformative festivals in Jainism. Celebrated primarily by Śvetāmbara Jains for eight days (Ashtāhnika) and by Digambara Jains for ten days (Daśalakṣaṇa), it is a time for deep self-reflection, discipline, study, and—most importantly—universal forgiveness.

At its heart, Paryushan is about purifying the mind through self-awareness and compassion. It invites us to pause, introspect, and realign our actions with the core Jain principles of Ahimsā (non-violence), Satya (truth), Asteya (non-stealing), Brahmacharya (chastity), and Aparigraha (non-possessiveness).

## What does “Paryushan” mean?
The word “Paryushan” comes from Sanskrit:
- "Pari" = all around, completely
- "Ushan" (from upa + vas) = to stay close, to dwell within

Together, Paryushan means “to stay with the self”—to turn inward, observe our thoughts, and purify our karma through right conduct and devotion.

## Duration and Traditions
- Śvetāmbara Jains: 8 days of Paryushan ending with the day of **Kṣamāvaṇī** (forgiveness)
- Digambara Jains: 10-day **Daśalakṣaṇa** Parva focusing on ten supreme virtues (like forgiveness, humility, purity, etc.)

Both traditions emphasize scriptural study, fasting, meditation, and asking forgiveness from all beings.

## Core Practices During Paryushan

### 1) Samayik (Equanimity)
A practice of sitting in stillness and cultivating balance of mind. Even 48 minutes a day can calm the mind and sharpen focus.

### 2) Pratikraman (Introspective Repentance)
A daily ritual of honestly reviewing one’s actions, acknowledging mistakes, and resolving to improve. It is a powerful tool for character-building.

### 3) Pratyākhyān (Vows of Restraint)
Taking small, practical vows—like reducing screen time, avoiding harsh speech, or limiting waste—builds self-control.

### 4) Svādhyāy (Scripture Study)
Reading and reflecting on Jain texts (e.g., Kalpasūtra during Śvetāmbara Paryushan) reinforces ethical understanding and right perspective.

### 5) Tap (Austerity/Fasting)
Fasting purifies the body and mind. Forms include:
- Ayambil (simple, spice-free meal once a day)
- Ekāsan (one meal a day)
- Biyasān (two meals a day without snacks)
- Upavās (total fast for a day or more)
- Proṣadh (observing fast on specific days with spiritual focus)

Always fast safely and with guidance; intent matters more than intensity.

### 6) Kṣamā (Forgiveness)
The culmination of Paryushan is seeking and granting forgiveness. The spirit is captured in the phrase:

> Micchāmi Dukkaḍaṁ — “If I have caused you harm in any way, knowingly or unknowingly, I seek your forgiveness.”

## The Ten Virtues of Daśalakṣaṇa (Digambara)
1. Kṣamā (Forgiveness)
2. Mardava (Humility)
3. Ārjava (Straightforwardness)
4. Śaucha (Purity)
5. Satya (Truth)
6. Saṁyam (Self-restraint)
7. Tapa (Austerity)
8. Tyāga (Renunciation)
9. Ākiñcanya (Non-attachment)
10. Brahmacharya (Chastity)

Reflecting on even one virtue per day can make Paryushan deeply transformative.

## A Simple Daily Routine for Students During Paryushan
- Begin with a short prayer or 5–10 minutes of Samayik (deep breathing and quiet sitting)
- Read a page from a Jain text or a quote on non-violence/compassion
- Practice mindful eating; avoid waste and overeating
- Speak gently; avoid gossip and arguments for the day
- Do Pratikraman at night—journal three things to improve tomorrow

## Student Corner: Living Paryushan Values in School Life
- Ahimsā in action: Use kind words, avoid bullying (offline/online), respect all life
- Aparigraha: Declutter your desk; donate books you no longer use
- Satya: Be honest in exams and projects—integrity over shortcuts
- Saṁyam: Set a daily phone/social media limit
- Seva: Help a classmate struggling with a subject

Small, consistent actions build strong character.

## Kṣamāvaṇī Message (You Can Share This)
If I have hurt you by words, actions, or thoughts—intentionally or unintentionally—please forgive me. I truly wish you peace, health, and progress. Micchāmi Dukkaḍaṁ.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is fasting compulsory?
No. The goal is inner purification. Choose a level suitable for your health and responsibilities. Even mindful eating and simple meals count.

### Can non-Jains observe Paryushan?
Yes. Values like compassion, truthfulness, and forgiveness are universal. Anyone can participate respectfully.

### What if I miss a ritual one day?
Return gently the next day. Paryushan is about sincerity, not perfection.

## Key Takeaways
- Paryushan is a festival of self-improvement through reflection, restraint, study, and forgiveness.
- Focus on intention over intensity; small vows practiced sincerely have a big impact.
- End with Kṣamāvaṇī—seek and grant forgiveness to lighten the heart and relationships.
